**Two roles, both equally important:**
- The **Explorer** (6 years old) always picks the right tool from the backpack.
- The **Tracker** (3 years old) always figures out for THEMSELVES where the trail leads — you give two simple options ("shall we go to the roller coaster, or to the castle?") and the Tracker points/chooses. That's a real choice, not a trick.

---

## Preparation: The Backpack

> "Before you set off on your adventure, the FunPark boss gives you a little backpack with four things in it: a little sword, a rope, a light, and a shovel. You may only take out ONE at a time when it's needed — so pay close attention to which one you pick!"

Give the Explorer the piece of paper with the four options. At every choice moment below, ask: **"Which of the four do you take now?"** and let the child point/choose before continuing.

---

## Introduction (read aloud with lots of funny voices)

> "Today you're back in Playmobil FunPark! But something strange is going on: Gnome Pim, who has a little house next to the roller coaster, has lost his magical candy pot. Without that pot, NOBODY in the park will ever get candy again!
>
> Pim comes running up to your figures: 'Help! Help! You, brave [Explorer's name], with your backpack full of tools — and you, clever [Tracker's name], who always finds the best clues — will you help me search through the park?'"

Let the children shout "YES!"

**The Tracker's first choice:** "Where shall we look for the first clue — near the roller coaster, or near the playground?" Let the youngest child really choose; then continue with the place that was chosen (the order below is just an example, feel free to reverse it).

---

## Location 1: The Tall Climbing Frame (near the roller coaster)

**Tracker moment first:** "Look around carefully — do you see anything strange, anything that doesn't belong here?" Let the Tracker point something out themselves (a glint, a splash of color, anything) and confirm enthusiastically: "Well spotted! That's it!"

> "Near the roller coaster you see something glinting all the way at the top of a tree/climbing frame — a ribbon from Pim's candy pot! It's hanging too high to just grab."

**Choice moment (Explorer):** "Which thing from the backpack could help here?" → **rope** (to pull something down, or throw over something).

> "Great choice! With the rope you manage to pull the ribbon down. Tied to the ribbon is a tiny little key — you'll need that later!"

**Next choice (Tracker):** "Which way does the trail smell/feel now — towards the castle, or towards the playground?" Let them choose and continue with that answer (adjust the order of Location 2 and 3 below if needed).

*(Wrong choice? No problem: "Hmm, that's not quite working — why don't you try the rope?" Keep gently steering, never really "losing".)*

---

## Location 2: The Dark Tunnel (near the castle/cave)

> "You walk to the castle part of the park and find a little tunnel that's completely dark. Something inside calls out softly: 'Come on in... if you dare!'"

**Choice moment:** "What do you need to be able to see in the dark?" → **light**.

> "With the light on you see: it's the bridge-keeper, a friendly little fellow who's a bit shy in the dark. 'Woohoo, how wonderful that you brought light! Now I can show you the way. But first — can you count to 5 for me? I've lost count of the steps.'"

**Extra mini-puzzle (counting, done together — the Tracker gets to start counting, the Explorer helps):** Count out loud together to 5 — for example 5 steps, 5 fingers, 5 hops.

> "'Hooray, well counted!' He lets you through."

**Next choice (Tracker):** "Do you smell the trail to the left, or to the right?" Let them choose and continue.

---

## Location 3: The Stuck Gate (near the playground)

> "At the end of the tunnel you see a small gate, but it's stuck fast, half buried in sand/grass."

**Choice moment:** "What could help loosen something from the sand?" → **shovel**.

> "With the shovel you dig the gate free. Behind it sits Mister Owl, who guards the lock: 'Woohoo! Nicely dug! Could you make one more sound for me — the sound of a chicken, and then a cow, very softly?'"

**Extra mini-puzzle (animal sounds):** the Tracker gets to choose a sound to imitate first, the Explorer does the second.

> "Mister Owl claps his wings, satisfied, and the gate creaks open."

**Last discovery (Tracker):** "Look around you — where do you think the candy pot could be hidden?" Let the Tracker "discover" the spot (e.g. behind a little tree, under a cushion) before moving on to the finale.

---

## Finale: The Candy Pot Found!

> "Behind the gate, on a soft cushion, stands the magical candy pot — with a little lock on it, exactly matching the key from Location 1!"

Let the Explorer "turn the key in the lock" (imaginary, or with a toy key). Make a big *click* sound.

> "The pot springs open with a POOF of glitter! Gnome Pim comes running: 'You did it — using the rope, the light, AND the shovel at exactly the right moment! You are the bravest heroes in all of FunPark! As a thank-you, everyone may choose ONE piece of candy — and the pot never runs empty, so there's always a new adventure waiting.'"

*(Tip: wrap up here with a small physical treat or sticker as a "reward" — and maybe the little sword, which never had to be used, can simply stay a "backup weapon for next time".)*

---

## Extra tips for while you play
- At every choice moment (for both children): let them guess/choose first. A wrong guess is no problem — just give a hint ("Hmm, maybe there's something else that fits better?") and let them choose again.
- The Tracker's choices (which way to go, what stands out) have no "wrong" answer — whichever route the child picks, you simply improvise the story in that direction. That way the choice always feels real.
- The little sword is deliberately never used in this story — that fits the "low combat" spirit. If your child asks about it anyway, you can say it's "for emergencies", and luckily this adventure didn't need one.
- Both figures are equally important now: the Explorer solves the tool puzzle, the Tracker decides the route and finds the clues. Switch consciously between them so no one feels left out.
- Use the real Playmobil figures and the park as your set: let them literally walk, climb, and count at the attractions that are already there.
